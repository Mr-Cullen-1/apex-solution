## Complete outline

The template supplies the H1. The opening paragraphs introduce the symptom, regional audience and forced-air scope. An early warning callout tells readers to evacuate for CO/gas emergencies before any checks.

1. **H2: How does a furnace heat your home?** Thermostat demand, heat generation, blower, ducts and returns; combustion exhaust separated from circulating air. Contrast gas, oil and electric resistance; older pilots versus electronic ignition.
2. **H2: Seven common reasons your furnace is not heating**
   - **H3: 1. Thermostat settings or a thermostat fault** — Heat mode, setpoint, schedule, replaceable batteries, Fan On versus Auto; no wiring work.
   - **H3: 2. A dirty or incorrectly fitted air filter** — Restricted airflow and possible safety shutdown; model-specific accessible filter care; no compartment access or repeated restarts.
   - **H3: 3. A power interruption or tripped circuit breaker** — Fuel-burning systems also need electricity; observe utility status and labeled controls; leave tripped breakers and unknown shutdowns for investigation.
   - **H3: 4. Ignition, pilot, or flame-detection problems** — Model-dependent gas components; report visible fault codes; no relighting, sensor cleaning or lockout resets.
   - **H3: 5. An interrupted fuel supply** — Natural-gas interruption versus delivered propane/oil; account or safe gauge observations; no valve operation, fuel-line work or self-restart after running dry.
   - **H3: 6. Blower motor or airflow problems** — Weak air/noise has overlapping causes; clear ordinary room obstructions; professional motor/capacitor/wiring work.
   - **H3: 7. A limit switch or another system component** — A safety trip is not proof of a defective switch; airflow, flame sensing, venting or condensing-system drainage may be involved; never bypass protections.
3. **H2: A safe homeowner troubleshooting checklist** — Five ordered steps: thermostat, accessible filter, room grilles, outage/fuel records, and symptom notes. Stop for tools, unfamiliar access or uncertainty. **H3: Winter check: snow and ice around outdoor vents** adds CPSC-backed ground-level observation, loose surrounding snow removal only when safely accessible, professional escalation and no roof/ladder access or vent dismantling.
4. **H2: When should you stop and call a professional?** — Persistent failure, repeated stopping, weak airflow or a tripped breaker. Prepare model/fuel/symptom notes. Move to warmth if needed; safe supplemental-heat boundaries.
5. **H2: Warning signs that require immediate attention** — CO is odorless; symptoms warrant evacuation without waiting for an alarm. Gas odor/hissing, smoke/fire/sparking, unexplained burning smells, soot or damaged venting. No unsafe restart.
6. **H2: Repair or replacement: what should you consider?** — Written diagnosis and comparable quotes; condition, repair history, parts, warranty, comfort, sizing and infrastructure. Bills and age alone do not decide the outcome.
7. **H2: Sources and further reading** ? Four reviewed CPSC/DOE destinations, also linked contextually in relevant paragraphs.
8. **H2: Get help with a furnace that is not working** — Article-specific non-emergency service links and accurate platform description.
9. **H2: Frequently asked questions** — Added by the existing article template from the FAQ field, using native disclosure controls. Questions below.
10. **Final CTA: A little guidance. A clear next step.** — The existing shared CTA follows FAQs/related services and supplies Book Now / Explore services. No extra CTA component or duplicate booking block added.

The template renders FAQ after body content; therefore the article-specific service paragraph precedes FAQ, and the shared final CTA follows it. This preserves the current schema and components.
