[6] Furnace Not Heating? 7 Common Causes and What to Check First
[7] Your house is getting cold, the thermostat says heat, and nothing is happening. Or the fan is running but the air coming out of the vents is cold. Sometimes it's a two-minute fix. Sometimes it's a part that needs a technician.
[8] This guide walks you through the most common reasons a furnace stops heating, what you can safely check yourself, and when to stop and call for help. It covers gas, oil and electric forced-air furnaces, the kind that blow warm air through vents. If your home has radiators or baseboard heat, you likely have a boiler instead. See our boiler repair page.
[9] Safety first. If you smell gas or your carbon monoxide alarm goes off, don't troubleshoot anything. Get everyone outside right away and call 911 or your gas utility (in New York that's usually Con Edison or National Grid) from outside. Don't flip switches, use the thermostat or unplug anything on the way out. Wait until the responders say it's safe to go back in.
[10] 7 common reasons your furnace isn't heating
[11] 1. The thermostat isn't asking for heat
[12] It sounds too simple, but it's one of the most common calls. Make sure the thermostat is set to Heat, the temperature is set a few degrees above the room temperature, and no schedule or vacation mode is overriding it. If it runs on batteries and the screen is blank or dim, replace them.
[13] Also check the fan setting. On On, the fan runs all the time, so you'll feel room-temperature air between heating cycles. Set it to Auto so it only runs when the furnace is heating.
[14] 2. The air filter is clogged
[15] A dirty filter chokes the airflow. Many furnaces will shut the burners off to protect themselves from overheating, so you get weak or no heat. Turn the furnace off, slide the filter out and hold it up to a light. If you can't see light through it, replace it with the same size, arrow pointing toward the furnace. In winter, check it every month.
[16] 3. The front panel isn't closed all the way
[17] Most furnaces have a safety switch behind the lower front panel. If the panel is loose or not seated properly, often right after someone changed the filter, the furnace won't start at all. Push the panel firmly into place and make sure it's latched.
[18] 4. The power is off
[19] A gas or oil furnace still needs electricity to run. Look for a switch near the furnace that looks like a regular light switch. It's easy for someone to flip it off by mistake, and if that's what happened, turn it back on. Then check your electrical panel. If the furnace breaker has tripped, you can reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off and call a technician, because something is causing it.
[20] 5. The vent pipes outside are blocked by snow or ice
[21] High-efficiency furnaces vent through two white plastic pipes that usually come out the side of the house. After a storm, snow, ice or even a bird's nest can block them, and the furnace shuts itself off as a safety measure. Go outside and gently clear any snow around the pipe openings by hand. Don't stick anything inside the pipes. This one is very common across Long Island, New Jersey and Connecticut after a heavy snowfall.
[22] 6. The burner won't light or stay lit
[23] If you hear the furnace click and try to start, then shut off, it could be the igniter, the flame sensor or the gas supply. On older furnaces with a pilot light, the pilot may be out. These parts need a technician. Don't try to relight a pilot or clean a sensor yourself, and don't keep restarting the furnace. Repeated restarts can lock it out. If there's a blinking light on the control board, note the pattern for the technician.
[24] If you heat with oil or propane, also check that the tank isn't empty. If it is, call your fuel supplier. The furnace will usually need to be restarted properly after a refill.
[25] 7. The blower or a safety switch has failed
[26] If the burners come on but little or no air comes out of the vents, the blower motor may have failed. If the blower runs but the air is cold, a high-limit switch may have shut the burners off because the furnace overheated, often because of a clogged filter or blocked return vent. A loud hum, grinding or squealing is another sign the blower needs attention. On high-efficiency furnaces, a clogged condensate drain can also shut the system down. All of these need a technician to find the real cause, not just reset the switch.
[27] When to stop and call a technician
[28] Call for service if:
[29] You've checked the thermostat, filter, panel, power and vent pipes, and there's still no heat
[30] The breaker trips again after you reset it
[31] The furnace starts, then shuts off again and again
[32] You hear grinding, squealing or banging you haven't heard before
[33] You smell something burning that doesn't go away after the first few minutes of the season
[34] You see soot, water pooling around the furnace or a damaged vent pipe
[35] You don't need to know what's broken before you call. Just tell us what the furnace is doing, what you already checked, and whether it runs on gas, oil or electric.
[36] If the house is getting too cold while you wait, never use the oven or a grill to heat it. A portable electric heater is fine if it's plugged straight into a wall outlet, kept at least three feet from anything that can burn, and turned off when you leave the room or go to sleep.
[37] Repair or replace?
[38] One breakdown doesn't mean you need a new furnace. Most no-heat calls come down to a single part: an igniter, a flame sensor, a switch or a blower motor. Ask the technician for a clear diagnosis and a written price for the repair before deciding anything.
[39] Replacement starts to make sense when the furnace keeps breaking down, needs a major part like a heat exchanger, or parts are no longer available for it. If you're comparing a repair with a replacement, ask exactly what each quote includes.
[40] Frequently asked questions
[41] Why is my furnace blowing cold air? The fan may be set to On instead of Auto, or the burners aren't lighting. If the setting is right and the air stays cold, it's usually an ignition, airflow or safety switch problem that needs a technician.
[42] Should I keep resetting my furnace? No. Resetting it once after a power outage is fine. If it keeps shutting off, something is triggering a safety lockout, and repeated resets can make it worse.
[43] Does every furnace have a pilot light? No. Most furnaces made in the last 20 years use electronic ignition. Older ones may still have a standing pilot.
[44] How often should a furnace be serviced? Once a year, ideally in the fall before the cold sets in. A tune-up catches worn parts early and keeps the furnace running efficiently. See our heating maintenance page.

[53] Refrigerator Not Cooling? Common Causes and What to Check First
[54] A warm fridge is one of those problems that can't wait. Food starts spoiling within hours, and it's not always obvious what went wrong. Sometimes the whole unit is warm. Sometimes the freezer is fine and only the fridge side won't get cold.
[55] Below are the checks you can do yourself in a few minutes, the problems technicians find most often, and how to tell when it's time to call for repair.
[56] Start with these quick checks
[57] Is it getting power? Open the door. If the light doesn't come on and the display is dark, check that the plug is pushed all the way in and that the breaker hasn't tripped.
[58] Check the temperature settings. Someone may have bumped the dial, or the fridge may be in a demo, vacation or "cooling off" mode. Set the fridge to 37°F and the freezer to 0°F. Your manual explains how to turn off demo mode.
[59] Look for blocked air vents. Cold air usually comes into the fridge from the freezer through vents on the back wall or top. A box or bag pushed against them can stop the airflow. Leave a few inches of space around the vents.
[60] Test the door seals. Close the door on a dollar bill. If it slides out with no resistance, the gasket isn't sealing in that spot. Also make sure nothing is keeping the door from closing fully.
[61] Clean the condenser coils. On most fridges the coils are behind a grille at the bottom front or on the back. When they're covered in dust or pet hair, the fridge can't get rid of heat. Unplug it and vacuum them with a brush attachment.
[62] Give it time. After a power outage, a move or a new installation, a fridge can take up to 24 hours to fully cool down.
[63] What it usually is when the quick checks don't help

[65] Evaporator fan
[66] This fan sits behind the back panel of the freezer and pushes cold air into the fridge. When it stops, the freezer can stay cold while the fridge warms up. A fan that's failing often gets noisy first, with buzzing or chirping that gets louder when you open the freezer door.
[67] Frozen coil and defrost problems
[68] Your fridge melts the frost off its cooling coil several times a day. If a defrost heater, thermostat or control fails, ice keeps building until it blocks the airflow. The fridge side usually warms up first. Unplugging the fridge for a day may get it working again for a while, but the ice will come back until the failed part is replaced.
[69] Air damper
[70] Many fridges use a small motorized flap to control how much cold air flows from the freezer into the fridge. If it sticks closed or freezes shut, the fridge side stays warm.
[71] Temperature sensor or control board
[72] The fridge relies on sensors to know when to cool. A bad sensor can make it think it's already cold enough, so it stops running. Control boards can fail too, especially after a power surge.
[73] Condenser fan
[74] On most modern fridges a second fan, next to the compressor at the bottom back, cools the condenser coils. If it stops, the fridge overheats and cools poorly. You may notice the bottom of the fridge feels unusually warm.
[75] Compressor and sealed system
[76] The compressor is the heart of the fridge. If it won't start, or the system has lost refrigerant from a leak, the fridge can run and run without getting cold. This is the most expensive kind of repair, so it's worth getting a proper diagnosis before you decide between fixing it and replacing the fridge.
[77] Keep your food safe in the meantime
[78] Keep the doors closed as much as you can. A closed fridge holds a safe temperature for about 4 hours.
[79] Put a thermometer inside. Food needs to stay at 40°F or below.
[80] Move milk, meat, fish and leftovers to a cooler with ice, or to a working fridge.
[81] Throw out perishable food that has been above 40°F for more than 2 hours. When in doubt, throw it out.
[82] When to call a technician
[83] Call for repair if the quick checks above didn't help, if the fridge is still warm after 24 hours, or if you notice any of these:
[84] The freezer is cold but the fridge isn't
[85] Ice keeps building up on the back wall of the freezer
[86] The fridge runs all the time, clicks repeatedly, or makes buzzing or grinding noises
[87] An error code keeps coming back
[88] Water is leaking inside or under the fridge
[89] Before you call, write down the brand and model number (usually on a sticker inside the fridge, along the side wall or ceiling), what's happening, and any error code. It helps the technician come prepared with the right parts.

[92] Frequently asked questions
[93] Why is my freezer cold but my fridge warm? Cold air for the fridge comes from the freezer. When the evaporator fan stops, the coil freezes over or the damper sticks, that air stops flowing, so the freezer stays cold while the fridge warms up.
[94] Should I unplug my fridge to reset it? Unplugging it for a few minutes can reset the control board after a power surge. If the coil is frozen, leaving it unplugged with the doors open for a day can melt the ice. If it works for a while and then stops again, a defrost part has failed and needs replacing.
[95] What temperature should my fridge be? 37°F for the fridge and 0°F for the freezer.
[96] Is it worth repairing my refrigerator? Most repairs on a fridge under 10 years old are worth it. For an older fridge with a failed compressor or a refrigerant leak, it's often smarter to compare the repair price with a new unit. Your technician can tell you which makes more sense.

[107] Commercial Refrigerator Not Cooling? What to Do Before You Lose Inventory
[108] When a reach-in, prep table or walk-in starts running warm, you're not just dealing with a repair. You're watching product, a health inspection and a busy shift all at once. A home fridge failing is a bad day. A walk-in failing on a Friday afternoon can cost a restaurant thousands.
[109] This guide is for restaurants, cafes, delis, bodegas, bars and offices. It covers what to do in the first hour, the most common reasons commercial units stop cooling, and how regular maintenance keeps it from happening again.
[110] First, protect your product
[111] Check the temperature with a separate thermometer. Don't rely only on the unit's display. In New York City, cold food has to be held at 41°F or below.
[112] Keep the doors closed. Every opening lets cold air out. Put a note on the door so staff don't keep checking.
[113] Move the most sensitive items first. Raw meat, fish, dairy and prepared foods go to a working unit or into coolers with ice.
[114] Log temperatures and times. If an inspector asks, a written record of what you did and when shows you handled it properly.
[115] Call for service early. The sooner a tech gets there, the more product you save. Don't wait for the unit to warm up completely.
[116] Why reach-ins and walk-ins stop cooling
[117] Dirty condenser coil
[118] This is the number one cause, especially in kitchens. Grease, flour and dust coat the coil, the unit can't release heat, and the compressor works harder and harder until it can't keep up. If you can reach the coil safely with the power off, brushing off loose dust helps. A proper coil cleaning every few months prevents most of these calls.
[119] Door gaskets and doors left open
[120] Torn or flattened gaskets let warm air in all day. So does a door that doesn't swing shut on its own, or a walk-in door with a broken closer or strip curtain. Look for frost or condensation around the door edges. That's a sign air is leaking.
[121] Blocked airflow inside
[122] Cases of product stacked against the evaporator fan or the back wall stop the cold air from circulating. In walk-ins, keep a few inches of space around the evaporator and don't stack product to the ceiling.
[123] Iced-up evaporator
[124] If the coil inside the unit is covered in ice, air can't pass through it. That usually points to a defrost heater, timer or drain problem. Shutting the unit off to melt the ice can buy time, but the ice will come back until the failed part is fixed.
[125] Fans, controls and sensors
[126] A failed evaporator or condenser fan, a bad temperature controller, or a faulty sensor can all stop cooling while the unit still looks like it's running. Units that were shut down for a while and then restarted are especially prone to controller problems.
[127] Refrigerant leak
[128] If the unit runs nonstop and never reaches temperature, it may be low on refrigerant. Leaks often happen at joints, the filter drier or the capillary tube. Finding and repairing a leak and recharging the system requires an EPA 608 certified technician.
[129] Call a technician right away if
[130] The unit is above 41°F and climbing
[131] The compressor is running constantly, or not running at all
[132] You see ice building up on the evaporator coil
[133] Water is leaking onto the floor
[134] The breaker keeps tripping
[135] You hear new grinding, rattling or loud buzzing
[136] Commercial ice machines
[137] Ice is food, so a dirty or failing ice machine is both a quality problem and a health code problem. Common signs your machine needs service:
[138] It's making less ice than usual, or smaller cubes
[139] The ice is cloudy, soft or tastes or smells off
[140] Water is leaking around the machine
[141] You see slime, mold or scale inside the bin or on the evaporator
[142] Most of these come down to maintenance. The industry standard is a full cleaning and sanitizing at least every six months, and more often if your water is hard or the machine sits in a hot, greasy kitchen. Water filters need regular replacement too, or scale builds up fast. We service Hoshizaki, Manitowoc, Scotsman and other major ice machine brands.
[143] Maintenance costs less than a breakdown
[144] Most emergency calls on commercial refrigeration trace back to skipped maintenance. A scheduled visit usually covers:
[145] Cleaning the condenser coil
[146] Checking door gaskets and hinges
[147] Checking fans, controls and temperatures
[148] Cleaning and sanitizing ice machines and replacing water filters
[149] Catching worn parts before they fail during a rush

[153] How often should commercial refrigeration be serviced? Condenser coils every few months in a busy kitchen, and a full check at least twice a year. Ice machines need cleaning and sanitizing at least every six months.

[165] Washer Not Draining or Spinning? Causes and Easy Fixes to Try First
[166] You open the washer and the clothes are sitting in water, or they come out soaking wet because the spin cycle never happened. Draining and spinning are connected: most washers won't spin until the water is out, so one problem usually causes both.
[167] The good news is that a lot of drain problems come down to a clog you can clear yourself. Here's where to start.
[168] Checks you can do yourself
[169] Restart the cycle on Drain & Spin. A power blip or a paused cycle can leave water behind. Run Drain & Spin on its own and see if it empties.
[170] Look at the drain hose. Pull the washer out a few inches and make sure the hose behind it isn't kinked, crushed or pushed too far down into the standpipe.
[171] Clean the drain pump filter (front-loaders). Most front-load washers have a small access door at the bottom front. Behind it is a filter that catches coins, hair ties, socks and lint. Put down towels and a shallow pan first, because water will come out. Unscrew the filter slowly, clean it and screw it back in tight.
[172] Check the lid or door. A washer won't spin if it can't lock the door or sense the lid is closed. Make sure nothing is caught in the door and that it latches firmly.
[173] Rebalance the load. One heavy item like a comforter or a bath mat can throw the drum off balance. Many washers will refuse to spin rather than shake. Spread the load out evenly and try again.
[174] Use less detergent. Too much soap, or regular detergent in a high-efficiency (HE) washer, creates suds that the washer reads as water. Use HE detergent and follow the dose on the bottle.
[175] If the display shows an error code (for example OE on many LG washers), write it down. It tells the technician where to start.
[176] What a technician usually finds
[TABLE 177]
What you notice | What it often points to

Won't drain or spin, no sound at all | Drain pump, control board or wiring
Loud banging during spin, then it stops | Worn shock absorbers, suspension rods or bearings
Drains slowly and smells | Partial clog in the pump, hose or house drain
[178] Drain pump
[179] The pump pushes water out of the washer. Small items that get past the filter can jam it, and pumps also simply wear out. If you hear humming but the water doesn't move, the pump is the usual suspect.
[180] Lid switch or door lock
[181] If the washer can't confirm the door is locked, it won't spin for safety. The latch can look fine and still have a failed switch inside.
[182] Belt, coupling and motor
[183] On many top-load washers a belt or a motor coupling connects the motor to the drum. When it breaks, the motor runs but the drum doesn't turn. A burning smell during spin is a sign to stop and call.
[184] Suspension and bearings
[185] Worn shocks or suspension rods let the drum bang around, and the washer stops spinning to protect itself. A loud rumble that gets worse at high speed often means the drum bearings are going.
[186] The house drain
[187] Sometimes the washer is fine and the standpipe or house drain is clogged. If water backs up out of the standpipe or a nearby sink drains slowly, you may need a plumber instead.
[188] Frequently asked questions
[189] Can I run another load while it's not draining? No. Running it again just adds more water and can overflow or damage the pump.
[190] How do I get the water out? On a front-loader, drain it slowly through the pump filter door into a pan, a little at a time. On a top-loader, scoop it out or lower the drain hose into a bucket on the floor.
[191] Is it worth fixing an older washer? Pumps, switches and belts are usually reasonable repairs. Bearings and motors cost more, so on an older washer it's worth comparing the price with a new one.

[199] Dryer Not Heating? Common Causes for Gas and Electric Dryers
[200] The drum is turning, the timer is counting down, and an hour later the clothes are still damp. A dryer that runs without heat is one of the most common appliance calls we get, and the cause is often airflow, not a broken heater.
[201] Before anything else, a word on safety. A clogged lint screen or vent doesn't just slow drying. Lint is very flammable, and lint buildup is a leading cause of dryer fires. If the dryer or the laundry room feels unusually hot, or you smell something burning, stop the dryer and unplug it.
[202] Checks you can do yourself
[203] Clean the lint screen. Do it before every load. If it looks clean but water beads on it, dryer sheet residue may be clogging the mesh. Wash it with warm soapy water and let it dry.
[204] Check the vent hose and outside vent. Pull the dryer out and look at the hose behind it. It shouldn't be crushed or kinked. Then go outside while the dryer runs. You should feel strong, warm air coming out of the vent hood. Weak or no airflow means the vent is clogged.
[205] Check the setting. Make sure it's not on Air Fluff, Air Dry or another no-heat cycle.
[206] Check the breaker (electric dryers). An electric dryer uses a double breaker. If only half of it trips, the drum can still turn but there's no heat. Flip that breaker fully off, then back on.
[207] Check the gas valve (gas dryers). The handle on the gas line behind the dryer should be parallel to the pipe. If you smell gas, don't touch anything. Leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.
[208] Don't overload it. A packed drum doesn't let air move through the clothes, and a big load can take twice as long.
[209] What a technician usually finds
[210] Blown thermal fuse
[211] The thermal fuse is a safety part that cuts the heat, or the whole dryer, when it overheats. It usually blows because the vent is clogged. Replacing the fuse without cleaning the vent means it will blow again, so a good technician checks both.
[212] Electric dryers: heating element
[213] The element is a coil of wire that heats the air. Over time it can break or short out. A dryer with a failed element runs normally but blows cool air.
[214] Gas dryers: igniter, gas valve coils or flame sensor
[215] In a gas dryer, an igniter glows to light the burner, then the gas valve coils open to let gas through, and a flame sensor confirms the flame. If the igniter glows but the burner never lights, or the flame lights and then goes out after a few seconds, one of these parts is usually the cause.
[216] Thermostats
[217] Cycling and high-limit thermostats turn the heat on and off to keep the temperature right. When one fails, you can get no heat or heat that cuts out too early.
[218] Control board or timer
[219] Less common, but on newer dryers a failed control board or timer can stop the heat even when every heating part is fine. Knobs that came off or don't turn properly can also be a sign of a timer problem.
[220] When to call
[221] You cleaned the lint screen and vent, checked the breaker or gas, and there's still no heat
[222] The dryer stops in the middle of a cycle

[457] Commercial Freezer Not Freezing? Causes and What to Do With Your Product
[458] A reach-in, chest or display freezer that won't hold temperature puts a lot of money at risk fast. Ice cream softens, meat starts to thaw and you have to decide what to keep. Freezers often give early warnings: frost on the door frame, ice on the back wall, a compressor that never stops, or a temperature that drifts up during the day.
[459] Protect your product first
[460] Check the real temperature. Put a thermometer in the middle of the freezer. Most commercial freezers should run at 0°F or colder.
[461] Keep the door closed. A full, closed freezer holds its temperature much longer than one that keeps getting opened.
[462] Move product to another freezer or pack it tightly together in coolers. Ice cream and seafood are the most sensitive.
[463] Know what you can keep. According to the FDA, thawed food can be safely refrozen if it still has ice crystals or is at 40°F or below. Anything above that for two hours or more should be thrown out.
[464] Log temperatures and times, then call for service.
[465] Quick checks
[466] Door and gaskets: Does the door close and seal on its own? Torn gaskets and doors that don't latch are the most common cause of icing and warm freezers.
[467] Frost on the door frame: Many freezers have a heater in the door frame to stop it freezing. Heavy frost or a door that sticks shut points to a failed heater or worn gasket.
[468] Airflow inside: Is product stacked against the fan or blocking the vents?
[469] Condenser: Is the coil, usually behind a grille at the bottom or top, covered in dust or grease? Is the unit pushed tight against a wall?
[470] Fans: Can you hear the fan inside the freezer and the one by the compressor?
[471] Power: Is it plugged in, and has a breaker tripped?
[472] What a technician usually finds
[TABLE 473]
What you notice | What it often points to
Ice building up on the back wall or coil | Defrost heater, defrost timer or controller, or warm air leaking in
Compressor runs nonstop, freezer never gets cold | Dirty condenser, refrigerant leak or failing compressor

[474] Defrost system
[475] Freezer coils need to defrost several times a day. When a defrost heater, timer or controller fails, ice keeps building until air can't flow through the coil. The freezer warms up even though the compressor is running. Melting the ice may buy you a day or two, but it comes back until the failed part is replaced.
[476] Dirty condenser
[477] The condenser coil gets rid of the heat pulled out of the freezer. In a kitchen it collects grease and dust fast. When it's clogged, the freezer can't reach temperature and the compressor overheats. Cleaning it every few months prevents a lot of breakdowns.
[478] Fans
[479] The evaporator fan moves cold air around inside the freezer, and the condenser fan cools the coil by the compressor. If either one stops, the freezer struggles to stay cold.
[480] Door gaskets and heaters
[481] Every time warm, humid air leaks in, it turns into frost on the coil. A torn gasket or a failed door frame heater keeps letting that air in all day.
[482] Refrigerant leaks and compressor problems
[483] If the freezer runs constantly and never gets cold, it may be low on refrigerant. Leaks often show up at joints, the filter drier or the capillary tube. Finding and fixing the leak and recharging the system has to be done by an EPA 608 certified technician. A failing compressor is the most expensive repair, so get a clear diagnosis and price before deciding whether to repair or replace.

[485] What temperature should a commercial freezer be? 0°F or colder for most frozen foods. Some operators run ice cream freezers colder.
[486] Why is my freezer icing up? Usually a door that isn't sealing, a defrost part that failed, or both.
[487] Can I just defrost it myself and keep using it? You can to get through a shift, but if a defrost part failed, the ice will come back within days. Get it checked.

[497] Commercial Ice Machine Not Making Ice? Common Causes and Quick Checks
[498] A bar or restaurant can go through a full bin of ice in a few hours. When the machine slows down or stops, you're buying bagged ice by the end of the day. The good news is that many ice machine problems come down to water, airflow or scale, and some of them you can check yourself.
[499] Quick checks before you call
[500] Is the water on? Check the shutoff valve on the line to the machine. If anyone worked on the plumbing recently, it may have been left closed.
[501] Is the water filter clogged? An old filter can choke the water supply and slow production. If it's been six months or more, it's probably due.
[502] Is the machine getting enough air? Air-cooled machines need space around the vents to breathe. Check the air filter, if your model has one, and rinse it if it's dusty or greasy. Don't stack boxes against the machine.
[503] Is the room too hot? Ice machines make much less ice in a hot kitchen or storage room, especially in summer. If production dropped when the weather got hot, heat may be part of the problem.
[504] Is the bin full or the sensor blocked? The machine stops when it thinks the bin is full. Ice piled against the bin sensor, or a dirty sensor, can stop it even when the bin isn't full.
[505] Is there an error light or beeping? Many machines show a light pattern or code when they shut down. Write it down for the technician.
[506] Has it been cleaned recently? If it's been more than six months, scale may be the problem. See our guide on how often to clean a commercial ice machine.
[507] What a technician usually finds
[TABLE 508]
What you notice | What it often points to
Makes some ice, but much less than before | Scale buildup, dirty condenser or air filter, clogged water filter, hot room
Ice forms but won't drop into the bin | Scale on the evaporator, harvest valve, sensor or control
Thin, cloudy or hollow cubes | Low water flow, scale, water pump or water level problem
No water flowing over the evaporator | Water inlet valve, water pump or supply problem
Machine runs but makes no ice at all | Refrigerant leak, compressor, condenser fan or control board
Water leaking around the machine | Clogged drain, cracked trough or a loose water line
[509] Scale buildup
[510] This is the most common cause of slow production. Minerals in the water coat the evaporator, so ice forms slowly and sticks instead of dropping. Descaling with the manufacturer's approved cleaner usually brings production back. If scale keeps coming back fast, a better water filter helps.
[511] Dirty condenser
[512] The machine has to get rid of heat to freeze water. When the condenser coil is coated in dust or kitchen grease, freezing slows way down and the compressor runs hot.
[513] Harvest problems
[514] At the end of each cycle, the machine warms the evaporator slightly so the ice releases. If a harvest valve, sensor or control fails, the ice stays stuck, gets thicker and the machine can shut itself down.
[515] Water pump, inlet valve and float or probe
[516] These parts fill the trough and pump water over the evaporator. If one fails, the machine can't get enough water to make full cubes, or any ice at all.
[517] Refrigerant and compressor
[518] If everything else checks out and the machine still won't make ice, it may be low on refrigerant or have a compressor problem. Leak repair and recharging must be done by an EPA 608 certified technician.
[519] Frequently asked questions
[520] Why is my ice machine making less ice in summer? Hotter air and warmer incoming water both slow down freezing. A clean condenser and good airflow around the machine help a lot.
[521] Why is my ice machine making ice but not dropping it? Usually scale on the evaporator or a harvest problem. Cleaning often fixes it. If not, a part needs to be checked.
[522] Should I unplug it and plug it back in? Once is fine if it shut down after a power issue. If it keeps stopping, write down any error light and call for service.
